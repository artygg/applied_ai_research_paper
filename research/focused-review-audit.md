# Focused confidence-and-engagement review

Revision date: September 11, 2026. This audit supersedes `ux-review-audit.md` for the current manuscript. Historical files preserve earlier scope and decisions, not independent verification of the current draft.

## Research scope

Title: **Generative AI in Duolingo: Feedback, Conversation Practice, and Learner Engagement**.

The manuscript now asks what research establishes about the effects of generative feedback and conversation on confidence and engagement. RQ1 compares features with conventional practice; RQ2 appraises direct versus transferable evidence; RQ3 addresses design and evidence limitations. Production and commercial priorities are confined to short Discussion context. Performance is retained to check whether perceived and demonstrated capability align, not as a separate comprehensive proficiency review.

The supplied 222-record collection remains a discovery inventory. This update is targeted documentary retrieval, not screening of all records or a registered systematic review. No learner observations, product tests, primary data reanalysis, or new meta-analysis were conducted. A framework and future evaluation proposals are explicitly analytical contributions.

## Consequential corrections

- The earlier manuscript's **280 + 105** split of the self-efficacy study's 385 learners was incorrect. The corrected final analysis populations are **153 + 232**. Recruitment and analysis stages are no longer combined. The original paper's Figures 4 and 8 and regression-table headings were inspected, including PDF screenshots.
- The earlier speaking-focused summary omitted **stable willingness to communicate and neutral confidence perceptions** in the Japanese-English report. These outcomes are now central to RQ2. A post-use confidence perception is not represented as a repeated pre/post confidence scale.
- Speaking post-test and post-survey populations are not conflated. The Japanese-English report has separate analysis denominators, recorded in `focused-evidence.csv`.
- The independent ChatGPT tutor article's abstract refers to random selection, but its methods describe **convenience sampling** and additional home/classroom practice. The paper uses the methods and does not label it a clean randomized chatbot-only intervention.
- The company's speaking-volume statement is classified as an **aggregate participation indicator**, not an independent experimental engagement effect. It comes from the May 4, 2026 shareholder letter, not a learner study.
- The January 16, 2025 Android announcement is a rollout event following the September 24, 2024 Video Call announcement. The manuscript also distinguishes the later introduction of broader subscriber access from original feature launch.
- The same Audrey Kittredge author's name is normalized to Audrey K. Kittredge across bibliography entries to avoid treating missing middle-initial metadata as a separate person.

## Wider evidence

`liMeta2025`, `saarela2026`, and `liTask2026` were rechecked against publisher material. The first remains abstract-level for detailed appraisal. The second's heterogeneity, small-study effects, and positive sensitivity findings are reported together. The third's task-planning/AI-literacy/assessment distinctions are retained, with its November 5, 2025 online date separate from its 2026 issue. The 144-study scope review `liReview2025` is retained from earlier publisher inspection only to contextualize population and duration coverage.

No common pooled effect for confidence, no new standardized estimate, and no sum of review participant totals were calculated. Within-review and between-review overlap was not independently reconstructed. Non-significant findings are not called evidence of equivalence. Positive average learning effects are not transferred to an individual Duolingo feature.
