1 Evidence claim Strong

Does Duolingo teach? Twenty years of efficacy evidence, sorted by who paid for it

How strong is the evidence that Duolingo produces measurable language gains, and how much of it is independent of Duolingo?

What the sources can carry

  • Characterise the full 2020-2026 empirical record on Duolingo learning outcomes.
  • Compare receptive against productive skill gains across independent studies.
  • Show that self-published efficacy reports and peer-reviewed studies use systematically different designs and outcome measures.
  • Test whether effect sizes differ by author affiliation - there are enough studies on both sides to try.

What they cannot

  • Produce a defensible pooled effect size: outcome instruments range from Versant to in-house placement scores to self-report.
  • Say anything about post-2023 generative features - almost no efficacy study covers them.

Method that fits

Systematic review extending Shortt et al. (2023), whose window closed at 2020. Code every study for design, sample, outcome measure and author affiliation; report Duolingo-authored and independent results separately rather than pooling them.

Watch for jiang24calico, the most-cited 'does Duolingo work' study, has mixed Duolingo/academic authorship, which is why it carries provenance: mixed. A sensitivity analysis with and without it is not optional.

2 Evidence claim Strong

Building a validity argument for an AI-scored high-stakes test

How much of the Duolingo English Test's validity and fairness case is externally peer-reviewed, and how much rests on the company's own technical reports?

What the sources can carry

  • Reconstruct the complete published validity argument from primary documents, including two technical manuals.
  • Set the in-house case against twelve independent validity, fairness and accent-bias studies.
  • Stage a real scholarly exchange: Burstein et al.'s responsible-AI case study against Johnson's direct rebuttal.
  • Trace a single construct - interactive reading, or speaking - from item-generation paper to whitepaper to independent critique.

What they cannot

  • Audit the scoring models. No item pool, no model weights, no subgroup error analysis beyond what the company reports.
  • Compare against IELTS or TOEFL internals, which are equally closed.

Method that fits

Validity-argument analysis in the Kane tradition. Map each inferential link - scoring, generalisation, extrapolation, decision - to the document that supports it, then mark each document peer-reviewed or self-published.

Watch for The 35 first-party DET sources will dominate any word count. Guard the ratio deliberately or the chapter becomes a summary of Duolingo's own case.

3 Evidence claim Strong

What open data bought: Duolingo's datasets and the research they made possible

What did releasing SLAM, STAPLE and the half-life regression traces produce, and what happened when the releases stopped?

What the sources can carry

  • Show a decade of external work - knowledge tracing, fairness auditing, spaced-repetition optimisation, MT robustness - built on Duolingo data.
  • Document that Duolingo's own model was beaten using its own data (tabibian19pnas) and independently replicated (avelar-hlr, papousek-hlr).
  • Date the last release precisely: 2020. Nothing from the generative era has been opened.
  • Argue open data as research infrastructure - a contribution that outweighs the company's own papers.

What they cannot

  • Quantify how much of the knowledge-tracing field depends on Duolingo data without a formal bibliometric pass this collection does not contain.
  • Explain why the releases stopped. No source states a reason.

Method that fits

Citation and reuse analysis of the four public datasets. Trace forward from each release into the downstream literature, then contrast with Birdbrain and the generative stack, where nothing was released.

Watch for Twelve independent peer-reviewed sources make this solid, but half predate 2020. Frame it as a historical argument about openness, not a claim about current practice.

4 Evidence claim Strong

Streaks, habits and the line between engagement design and dark patterns

Does the motivational architecture that drives Duolingo's retention also drive learning, and where does the critical literature say it stops helping?

What the sources can carry

  • Set a peer-reviewed habit-formation case study and a critical gamification analysis against the company's own optimisation paper and its CEO's stated goal.
  • Use the dose-response and attrition studies to connect engagement mechanics to actual learning time.
  • Make an argument about engagement optimisation as an AI application in its own right.

What they cannot

  • Claim causation between specific mechanics and dropout. The designs are correlational or qualitative.
  • Cover the current recommendation stack, which is undocumented.

Method that fits

Thematic synthesis of the critical and motivational literature, anchored on the one first-party technical artefact that shows engagement being optimised directly: the notification bandit paper.

Watch for Thematically coherent but assembled across six themes. The AI connection is real - the bandit paper - but it has to be argued, not assumed.

5 Corpus study Strong

The frozen shop window: where Duolingo's research output went after 2021

What does the venue and volume of Duolingo's own publications reveal about where AI is load-bearing for the business?

What the sources can carry

  • Demonstrate the portal has listed nothing since McCarthy et al., EMNLP 2021, while the same teams published at BEA, EMNLP, ACL Findings, PMLR and on arXiv through 2026.
  • Quantify the reallocation from consumer-app research to assessment research.
  • Show the whitepaper working as a genre: substantive, versioned, technically detailed, and outside peer review.
  • Trace individual researchers across the shift using their public profiles.

What they cannot

  • Claim the portal freeze was deliberate. It is equally consistent with neglect.
  • Count unpublished internal research.

Method that fits

Bibliometric analysis of the first-party corpus by venue, year, theme, peer-review status and author. Compare what the public research portal lists against what the same authors actually published.

Watch for Original work - nobody has published this analysis - which also means no prior methodology to lean on. Choose the bibliometric frame explicitly and state the corpus boundary.

6 Corpus study Strong

Automatic item generation and automated scoring as an applied NLP problem

What are the actual methods behind AI-driven test construction and scoring, and how did they change as LLMs arrived?

What the sources can carry

  • Give a complete, well-documented technical account - this is the best-documented part of the whole Duolingo AI story.
  • Show a clear methodological progression across a decade with named architectures and reported metrics.
  • Connect the industrial work to the academic knowledge-tracing and question-generation literature.

What they cannot

  • Verify reported results. No code or data accompanies the post-2020 papers.
  • Cover the consumer app's models, which have no method papers at all.

Method that fits

Technical review. Read the method sections and trace the arc from logistic regression over hand-built features, to BERT-IRT, to GPT-4-based generation and scoring, noting which claims rest on held-out evaluation.

Watch for Almost entirely Duolingo-authored, and the peer-reviewed portion sits in workshop venues. Strong as a methods chapter, weak as evidence of quality.

7 Evidence claim Workable

The field's default exemplar: what it costs to study one company's product

Why is Duolingo simultaneously over-used as a case study and under-studied as a system, and what does that do to the field's conclusions?

What the sources can carry

  • Cite a 2026 twenty-year scoping review finding Duolingo in 1.4% of empirical AI-in-language-learning studies.
  • Draw on a published methodological critique of research using commercial apps.
  • Use this collection's own provenance distribution as evidence - 107 Duolingo-authored against 80 independent - which is a defensible original contribution.
  • Note that the company funds an external research grant programme.

What they cannot

  • Prove that funding shaped findings. Nothing here supports that, and implying it would be unfair.
  • Generalise to Babbel, Busuu or Memrise. There is one comparative study.

Method that fits

Methodological critique that uses the existing reviews as data: how often Duolingo appears, in what role, with what author affiliations, and with what access to the system under study.

Watch for Reflexive: the collection is both instrument and evidence. State that openly and describe how the inventory was built, or the argument is circular.

Three years of Duolingo Max without an independent outcome study

What outcome evidence exists for the generative-AI features, and what explains the gap between shipping date and evidence date?

What the sources can carry

  • Document the gap precisely, with dated primary sources on both sides.
  • Show what a company publishes while it has no outcome evidence yet.

How the rating works

Load-bearing sources

A source counts as load-bearing when it is peer-reviewed and written by someone with no Duolingo affiliation. Duolingo-authored work is not discounted as evidence about the company; it is discounted as independent corroboration of the company's claims. The three sources with mixed authorship never count as load-bearing, which is why they carry their own tag.

Ratings run on four bands. An evidence claim reaches strong at twelve load-bearing sources across a base of fifteen or more; a corpus or discourse study reaches it on volume, first-party depth and a span of at least four years. The full scoring rule is in analyse.py, and the counts here are computed from the inventory rather than estimated.

Four kinds of claim

Evidence claim
Argues that something is true about learners or scores. Needs sources Duolingo did not write.
Corpus study
Argues about what was published, claimed or built. First-party documents are the primary data, not weak evidence.
Absence claim
Argues that evidence is missing. Judged on how completely the record of what does exist can be pinned down.
Discourse study
Argues about how something was said and received. Primary and contemporaneous sources are the data.
