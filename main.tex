\section*{Abstract}
Since 2023, generative AI has entered Duolingo along two routes: learners interact with generated explanations and conversations, and the company uses language models to produce course content at scale. This paper asks how generative AI transforms the Duolingo user experience. A focused documentary review of company documentation and filings, Duolingo-specific studies, and field-level research syntheses, checked against original sources up to September 2026, addresses three sub-questions: what changed relative to the pre-generative app, what can be shown about effects on learners, and whether the change enriches or industrialises the experience. A dated timeline shows learner-facing features (Explain My Answer, Roleplay, Video Call) and production uses (AI-assisted lessons, DuoRadio, 148 new courses) developing in parallel. Field-level research reports positive but heterogeneous effects of generative AI on language learning, whereas Duolingo-specific evidence consists mainly of short, company-authored studies with mixed confidence findings and a few small external papers. The review concludes that generative AI enriches the range of practice while industrialising its production, and that public evidence is considerably stronger for scale, cost, and activity than for learning, motivation, or confidence.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Duolingo; generative AI; user experience; language learning apps; learner engagement; AI-generated content.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\subsection{Reason for the Investigation}
Duolingo is a useful case for studying how generative AI changes a consumer learning product, because the company adopted it early, publicly, and on two fronts. In March 2023 it introduced Duolingo Max, a subscription tier with GPT-4-based explanations and roleplay conversations \parencite{maxLaunch}. In June 2023 it described how language models help its staff create lessons \parencite{contentProduction}, and in April 2025 it launched 148 new courses built with generative AI \parencite{courses2025}. The same technology therefore changed both what learners can do in the app and how the content they study is made.

Generative AI did not arrive in an app without artificial intelligence. Before 2023, Duolingo already personalised practice, and it used a bandit algorithm to decide which of its hand-written reminder notifications to send \parencite{freeman2023,yancey2020}. The question is therefore not whether AI entered Duolingo, but what generation adds to an experience that was already algorithmically personalised and gamified, and for whose benefit.

\subsection{Subject and Importance}
In this paper, the \emph{user experience} has three layers: the interactions available to learners, the content they encounter, and the incentives that shape when and how long they use the app. Generative AI can alter all three. A generated explanation or conversation changes the interaction; generated sentences, stories, and audio episodes change the content; and the metrics a company optimises determine which of these changes are pursued and how they are evaluated.

The topic matters beyond one company. Duolingo's management reported 23\% year-on-year growth in daily active users in the second quarter of 2026 \parencite{earnings2026}, so design decisions reach a very large learner population. Educators increasingly recommend such apps, yet a 2025 review of empirical research on generative AI in language learning found the evidence concentrated in higher-education and English-as-a-foreign-language settings rather than self-directed app use \parencite{liReview2025}. Duolingo's public commitment to AI also makes it a reference case for other education technology companies. If its changes are judged by press releases rather than evidence, that standard can spread.

\subsection{Research Questions and Objective}
The objective is to describe how generative AI has changed the Duolingo experience, to assess what the available evidence shows about its effects on learners, and to evaluate whose interests the changes primarily serve.

\noindent\textbf{Main research question}

How does generative AI transform the Duolingo user experience?

\noindent\textbf{Sub-questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What generative-AI features has Duolingo deployed since 2023, and how do they differ from the pre-generative user experience?
    \item What evidence exists that these features change learners' motivation, confidence, and engagement, and how much of it concerns Duolingo specifically rather than being inferred from the wider generative-AI literature?
    \item Does generative AI enrich the Duolingo experience or industrialise it, and whose outcomes is the system optimised for?
\end{enumerate}

The sub-questions build on each other: RQ1 describes the change, RQ2 appraises the evidence about its effects, and RQ3 uses both to construct an argument.

\subsection{Scope and Reading Guide}
The review covers Duolingo's consumer language courses from 2023 to September 2026. It includes both learner-facing generative features and generative content production, because both shape what learners experience. The Duolingo English Test, the mathematics, music, and chess courses, and labour relations are excluded. Technical architecture is discussed only where it explains the experience. Section~\ref{sec:method} explains how sources were selected and appraised, Section~\ref{sec:results} answers the three sub-questions in turn, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-discussion} answers the main question, evaluates the method, and makes recommendations.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Design, Population, and Sample}
This study is a focused documentary review. Its population is the public record on generative AI in Duolingo: company announcements, product and engineering documentation, investor communications, research about Duolingo, and research syntheses on generative AI in language learning. No learners were recruited, no accounts were tested, and no model outputs were scored. The unit of analysis is a document, and the review compares what documents claim with the kind of evidence they supply.

A collection of 222 annotated records on Duolingo and AI, compiled beforehand from Duolingo's research, blog, and investor pages, academic databases, preprint servers, and press coverage, served as the sampling frame. Sources were selected purposively when they addressed one of the three sub-questions, giving a sample of 45 cited sources. Purposive selection was preferred to exhaustive screening because the questions concern one product, and many records in the frame address other topics, such as the Duolingo English Test.

\subsection{Data Collection}
Each selected source was retrieved from its original publisher, company, or author-hosted location in September 2026 and checked against the claim for which it is cited. Where full text was unavailable, the accessible abstract or indexed passages were used, and claims were limited to those passages; this applies to several syntheses and working papers \parencite[e.g.,][]{harrison2025,espinosa2026,saarela2026}.

Dates were recorded at event level. An announcement, a platform rollout, a later technical description, and a change in subscription access were treated as separate events, because a study can only evaluate the version that existed when its data were collected. Live webpages can be edited after publication, so dated releases and filings were preferred for timing claims.

\subsection{Instruments}
Two instruments structured the review. A timeline form recorded, for each change, its date, its event type, whether it acted on learner-facing interaction or on content production, and the source type. An appraisal form recorded, for each study or synthesis, the population, the intervention and whether a generative feature was confirmed, the comparison, the outcome measure, the duration, and the authors' relationship to Duolingo. Four appraisal questions were applied: does the exposure match the claim; does the comparison support attribution; does the measure capture the stated outcome; and does the sample support generalisation to Duolingo users?

\subsection{Analysis}
Analysis followed the three sub-questions. For RQ1, events were ordered chronologically and compared with a baseline reconstructed from documentation and research on the pre-generative app. For RQ2, evidence was classified along two dimensions: \emph{specificity} (a confirmed Duolingo generative feature, general Duolingo use, or generative AI outside Duolingo) and \emph{independence} (company-authored or external). Motivation, confidence-related self-report, behavioural engagement, and language performance were kept separate rather than merged into one effect. For RQ3, the results of RQ1 and RQ2 were organised into a ledger contrasting indicators of enrichment with indicators of industrialisation and recording which outcomes each source actually measures.

\subsection{Justification of Methodological Choices}
A documentary review suits the questions because the relevant changes are publicly documented and a controlled learner study was not feasible within the available time. Separating specificity from independence prevents two common errors: treating positive field-level findings as proof for one product, and dismissing company research merely because of its authorship. Company documentation is used to establish what was offered and when, but not whether it worked. No quality score or pooled estimate was calculated, because the included studies differ too much in design and outcome to be combined meaningfully.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: What Changed, and Against What Baseline?}
\label{sec:rq1}
\subsubsection{The Pre-Generative Baseline}
Before March 2023, Duolingo was already an AI-supported, gamified app. Its January 2023 method report emphasises structured activity, personalisation, and sustained participation \parencite{freeman2023}. A systematic review of Duolingo research from the app's 2012 public release to early 2020 shows that researchers had long approached it through its gamification \parencite{shortt2023}. Engagement was also algorithmically optimised. A bandit algorithm chose among hand-written reminder templates and counted a notification as successful when the learner completed a lesson within two hours; in deployment it raised daily active users by 0.5\% and new-user retention by 2\% \parencite{yancey2020}.

Studies of general app use describe what this conventional experience offered. \textcite{liBonk2025}, whose article was submitted in 2022, interviewed ten self-directed learners who combined Duolingo with other resources. \textcite{zeng2024} followed 20 Chinese Year~8 students who used the app after school for six weeks and found that motivation for the app activity, together with satisfied needs for autonomy and competence, could transfer to broader motivation for learning English. \textcite{zhao2024} randomly assigned Chinese students in the United Kingdom to five weeks of Duolingo (\(n = 33\)) or HelloTalk (\(n = 34\)) to examine willingness to communicate, and \textcite{ouyang2024} studied 80 English learners using the app over two months in online classes. Although the latter describe Duolingo as an AI-integrated technology, none of these studies establishes exposure to a generative feature. For AI tools in general, a review of 18 studies found that gains in learner independence depended on combining the tools with explicit self-regulation instruction and structured scaffolding \parencite{mohebbi2025}.

Together, these sources characterise the baseline as pre-authored exercises in an adaptive sequence, correctness-based feedback, and gamified mechanisms that encourage learners to return. Learners could be motivated and practise regularly, but their contributions were largely confined to formats written in advance by course designers. This is an analytical characterisation, not a claim that the earlier app offered no speaking practice or explanations.

The interaction track began with the Max launch on 14 March 2023, which introduced Explain My Answer and Roleplay \parencite{maxLaunch}. Explain My Answer lets a learner request an explanation of a response that takes the error and the learner's language background into account \parencite{explainFree}. Roleplay is not a single open chatbot prompt: a sequence of specialised prompts receives the scenario, character, learning objective, and proficiency level, while application logic manages the stages of the exchange \parencite{roleplayDesign}. Video Call, announced on 24 September 2024 \parencite{duocon2024}, extends this to speech. Learning Designers write the system instructions that govern the character Lily; each call moves through an opener, a first question, free conversation, and a closer, is pitched at a CEFR level, and can draw on facts extracted from earlier call transcripts \parencite{henry2025}. A guided variant with the character Falstaff, originally aimed at beginners, adds suggested phrases, translations, and feedback \parencite{falstaff}.

The production track ran in parallel. A June 2023 account describes a Learning Designer planning a lesson's theme, grammar, vocabulary, and exercise types, a model producing ten candidate exercises within seconds, and the designer choosing and editing three of them, with teaching experts keeping the final say \parencite{contentProduction}. DuoRadio, a listening feature launched in late 2023, was scaled during a 2024 hackathon by generating scripts from existing curriculum content and filtering them with designer-written evaluation prompts for naturalness, grammaticality, coherence, and logic. The company reports growth from 300 to more than 15,000 episodes and from 2 to more than 25 courses, a 99\% cost saving, and daily sessions rising from 500,000 to 5 million in under six months \parencite{duoradio}. Duolingo researchers have also published a method for controlling the proficiency level of generated text, although the paper does not show that the method is used in the product \parencite{malik2024}. When 148 courses built with generative AI were released on 30 April 2025 \parencite{courses2025}, the chief executive contrasted about twelve years for the first 100 courses with about one year for nearly 150 new ones; the courses mainly targeted beginners and included Stories and DuoRadio \parencite{malikTechcrunch2025}.

Access changed as well as capability. Explain My Answer was announced as free from 1 January 2026 \parencite{explainAccess2025}, and in 2026 Video Call began to reach the cheaper Super tier \parencite{shareholderQ12026}. Management linked this to cost: a video call that initially cost about \$0.30 was reported to cost under \$0.01 after a move towards open-source models \parencite{earnings2026}.

\subsubsection{How the Experience Differs}
Compared with the baseline, the interaction track introduces three changes. First, feedback moves from \emph{whether} an answer is correct to \emph{why}: a learner can obtain a contextual reason rather than only a correction. Second, practice moves from selecting among pre-authored options to producing open responses that the system answers. In Roleplay and Video Call, the learner's own utterance shapes the next turn, which fixed exercise formats cannot do. Third, the experience becomes partly persistent, because facts from earlier calls can shape later conversations \parencite{henry2025}. Guidance adds a fourth, less obvious change. Because guided calls offer suggested phrases and translations \parencite{falstaff}, a beginner can produce a first turn that would otherwise be out of reach, but a learner who repeatedly copies suggestions can also complete a conversation without producing language independently. Completing a task therefore means something different in a guided call than in a fixed exercise, and it cannot by itself show what the learner can do.

The production track changes the experience differently. Learners do not choose generated content; they meet it as ordinary lessons, stories, or episodes. The change is therefore largely invisible at the moment of use, but it determines which courses exist, how much listening material is available, and how quickly new content appears. Even researchers studying generated lessons could not confirm which of the lessons their participants had seen were generated \parencite{catalan2026}.

Several elements did not change. Lessons remain structured and gamified, streaks and reminders still encourage daily return, and the generative features operate within designer-written instructions rather than as unrestricted chatbots \parencite{roleplayDesign,henry2025}. The transformation is thus an extension of an existing adaptive, gamified product, not its replacement.

\subsubsection{Why the Dates Matter}
Precise dating determines which claims RQ2 can support. Evidence collected before 14 March 2023 cannot evaluate any generative feature, and evidence from 2023 cannot evaluate Video Call, announced in September 2024. Studies of general app use describe the conventional experience to which generative features were added, not the features themselves. Likewise, a finding about Explain My Answer as a paid feature may not hold after it became free, because the population of users changed. The self-efficacy study discussed below illustrates this: its data were collected in late 2023, when Roleplay and Explain My Answer were available to paying subscribers and to learners given access for the study, before Explain My Answer became free and before Video Call existed \parencite{kittredge2025self}. Each study in RQ2 is therefore read against the version and access conditions that applied when its data were collected.

\subsection{RQ2: What Can Be Shown About the Effects on Learners?}
\label{sec:rq2}
\subsubsection{Outcomes and Evidence Standards}
Three outcomes are examined. \emph{Motivation} concerns reasons for learning and persisting. \emph{Confidence} covers perceived ability, including task-specific self-efficacy and willingness to communicate; neither is a proficiency score. \emph{Engagement} follows the distinction between behavioural participation, emotional involvement, and cognitive investment used in a systematic review of 72 ChatGPT studies \parencite{loEngagement2024}. A feature can increase activity without deepening cognitive engagement, and a learner can feel more confident without performing better. For each outcome, the review asks whether the evidence concerns a confirmed Duolingo generative feature and who produced it.

\subsubsection{Field-Level Evidence Is Substantial}
Research on generative AI in language learning has grown quickly. A systematic review of 144 empirical articles from 2023 and 2024 found that 86.7\% of studies took place in higher education and 86.1\% in English-as-a-foreign-language contexts. Writing was the most frequent focus, at 42.4\%, while speaking, listening, and reading received less attention, and longitudinal studies were scarce \parencite{liReview2025}.

Quantitative syntheses report positive average effects. A meta-analysis of generative-AI chatbots included 41 studies with 3,515 participants and reported a pooled effect of 0.576 (95\% CI [0.385, 0.768]) on second-language acquisition \parencite{liMeta2025}. A second meta-analysis of 51 studies found large positive effects on language proficiency and on affective--cognitive outcomes, but with very high heterogeneity (\(I^2 = 98.4\%\)) and significant small-study effects. Its sensitivity analyses retained the main conclusions, and its moderator analyses indicated stronger effects in informal learning settings, for productive skills, and for less commonly taught languages \parencite{saarela2026}. A meta-analysis of 25 task-based studies with 2,431 participants identified AI literacy, task planning, and assessment arrangements as potential moderators \parencite{liTask2026}. For earlier, non-generative personalisation, a meta-analysis of 61 samples from 17 research projects, which named Duolingo among the platforms studied, reported a between-group effect of \(d = 0.39\) and a larger within-group effect of \(d = 1.18\) \parencite{lee2024}.

These results make the proposed mechanisms plausible. Generative conversation and feedback can improve learning and affective outcomes in many studied settings, and the informal-setting and productive-skill moderators are, if anything, favourable to an app offering spoken conversation. For this paper, however, the syntheses have clear limits. Their participants are mostly university students in instructed settings, the interventions are usually general tools such as ChatGPT embedded in teaching, and pooled effects combine very different designs. None estimates the effect of a Duolingo feature, and their participant totals cannot be added because reviews overlap.

\subsubsection{Duolingo-Specific Evidence Is Thin}
The closest evidence comes from Duolingo itself. \textcite{kittredge2025self} studied self-efficacy after one month of access to Roleplay and Explain My Answer, with data collected between 30 August and 17 November 2023. Final analyses included 153 existing subscribers and 232 learners newly given access. Significant improvement was found for one of seven analysed self-efficacy items in the existing-user cohort and for six in the new-access cohort. There was no control group without the features, seven of seventeen collected items were selected for analysis, six-point responses were dichotomised, and feature-use counts did not significantly predict post-survey self-efficacy. Usage requirements selected adherent participants, and all authors were affiliated with Duolingo.

A company report on Japanese learners of English followed 658 learners into two 30-day conditions, with 567 included in the speaking post-test analysis and 558 in the survey analysis. Regular Video Call users showed a small speaking-performance advantage over learners using regular lessons, but willingness to communicate remained stable and confidence perceptions after using the feature were neutral. The call condition required at least two calls a day, and exclusions were more frequent in that condition \parencite{kittredge2025video}. A later company summary reports that English speakers learning early-intermediate Spanish improved their speaking more and reported higher confidence with Lily than with regular lessons, but it gives too little detail on sample, assignment, or instruments for full appraisal \parencite{videoResearch}. A summary of the guided Falstaff calls reports higher scores on spoken translation of practised phrases among beginners who used the calls, which is evidence about phrase production rather than confidence \parencite{falstaffResearch}. At the behavioural level, the company reported that average words spoken per user in Video Call more than doubled over a year \parencite{shareholderQ12026}; without a fixed version or population, this indicates participation, not learning.

Independent research is scarcer. \textcite{harrison2025} reviews Video Call alongside other conversational tools, but the accessible abstract identifies it as a technology review rather than a learner study. \textcite{catalan2026} surveyed five software engineers in the Philippines who were learning Korean. They found general scenarios helpful for grammar and vocabulary, but rarely encountered work-related scenarios and wanted more personalised content; the authors did not verify which lessons were generated. \textcite{xu2025} compare Duolingo with ChatGPT on motivation, enjoyment, critical thinking, and autonomy, but the accessible material does not establish which Duolingo features or version participants used, so the study cannot be treated as evidence about generative features.

\subsubsection{What Comparable Studies Add}
Studies of similar generative conversation outside Duolingo help to interpret the mixed confidence findings. \textcite{wangConversation2024} compared three classes of 33 Chinese undergraduates who practised with a teacher and peers, with a text-and-voice chatbot, or with an avatar-based generative chatbot. The avatar condition improved willingness to communicate and self-perceived competence and reduced speaking anxiety relative to the teacher-and-peer group, without a significant advantage in speaking performance. This is the mirror image of the Japanese Video Call result, in which performance improved but confidence perceptions did not. \textcite{celik2025} reported higher speaking self-efficacy after an eight-week ChatGPT-supported intervention with 44 university learners, but their methods describe convenience sampling and additional home practice and training, so the difference cannot be attributed to the chatbot alone. Neither study concerns Duolingo, but together they show that affective and performance outcomes can diverge in either direction and should be measured together.

The setting also matters. In a study of 60 learners using a generative chatbot, those with a teacher present reported higher emotional and agentic engagement than those without, whereas behavioural and cognitive engagement did not differ significantly \parencite{liTeacher2026}. Most field-level studies take place in classes where teachers can explain feedback and hold learners accountable, while Duolingo learners typically practise alone. Benefits observed in supported classrooms cannot be assumed to reproduce in unsupervised app use.

\subsubsection{Motivation, Confidence, and Engagement Compared}
Table~\ref{tab:evidence} maps this evidence against specificity and authorship. The pattern differs by outcome.

For \emph{motivation}, no inspected study measures the effect of a confirmed Duolingo generative feature. Evidence that Duolingo supports motivation comes from general app use \parencite{zeng2024}, and evidence that generative AI supports affective outcomes comes from outside Duolingo \parencite{saarela2026}. The claim that Duolingo's generative features motivate learners is therefore an inference combining two bodies of evidence, neither of which tests it directly.

For \emph{confidence}, direct evidence exists but is mixed. Selected Roleplay and Explain My Answer users reported higher self-efficacy on some items, without a control group \parencite{kittredge2025self}; Japanese learners showed stable willingness to communicate and neutral confidence perceptions despite a performance benefit \parencite{kittredge2025video}; and a less detailed summary reports higher confidence among Spanish learners \parencite{videoResearch}. The mix is itself informative: confidence and performance need not move together.

For \emph{engagement}, behavioural indicators are the strongest part of the direct record, since spoken words per user increased \parencite{shareholderQ12026}. Emotional and cognitive engagement with the generative features, such as whether learners interpret explanations or repair misunderstandings, is not measured in the inspected sources. Field research advises caution: evidence on cognitive engagement with ChatGPT is broad but weak \parencite{loEngagement2024}, and in a small case study favourable reactions to ChatGPT feedback did not ensure that learners used the feedback effectively \parencite{yan2024}.

\subsubsection{The Evidence Asymmetry}
The central finding of RQ2 is an asymmetry. At field level, generative AI in language learning is supported by several meta-analyses and systematic reviews reporting positive but heterogeneous effects. At Duolingo level, the inspected evidence on learner outcomes consists of one peer-reviewed, company-authored self-report study, a company research report with mixed results, shorter company summaries, an aggregate usage statistic, and a few small or access-limited external papers. None of the direct studies follows learners for longer than about a month, and none is an independent controlled evaluation of a generative feature.

The design details of the direct studies further limit what their positive results mean. When learners must use a feature regularly before follow-up, the results describe those who kept using it, not everyone who was offered it; for engagement, the learners who stop are part of the outcome. Converting six-point ratings into two categories discards differences in the strength of confidence, and a statistically significant change on a questionnaire item is not necessarily a meaningful change for a learner. Equally, the non-significant association between feature use and self-efficacy does not show that use is irrelevant; it shows only that this design did not detect a relationship.

This asymmetry should be stated rather than smoothed over, and it cuts both ways. It does not show that the features are ineffective, and the company studies are more candid than marketing: the Japanese report records neutral confidence perceptions, and the self-efficacy study reports that feature use did not predict outcomes. Nor can field-level results be transferred to Duolingo without qualification, because the populations, settings, and tools differ. The defensible answer to RQ2 is that Duolingo's generative features plausibly support confidence and engagement for some learners, but most of what is claimed about their effects is inferred from other settings rather than shown for Duolingo users.

\subsection{RQ3: Enrichment or Industrialisation?}
\label{sec:rq3}
\subsubsection{Two Readings of the Same Change}
Here, \emph{enrichment} means that generative AI gives learners forms of practice or support that were previously unavailable to them, judged by learner outcomes. \emph{Industrialisation} means that generative AI turns content creation and interaction into standardised, high-volume production, judged by throughput, cost, and usage. The two readings are not mutually exclusive. Figure~\ref{fig:tracks} shows how both tracks from RQ1 feed into a gamified app whose routinely reported outcomes differ from its educational ones.

\begin{figure*}[t]

\subsubsection{The Case for Enrichment}
The strongest case for enrichment lies in the interaction track. Open spoken conversation at any time, with suggested phrases for beginners and designer-written instructions for level and topic, is a kind of practice the pre-generative app did not offer \parencite{henry2025,falstaff}. Explanations that address a learner's specific error add a reason to a correction \parencite{explainFree}. Access has also widened: Explain My Answer became available to free users, and Video Call moved into a cheaper subscription tier \parencite{explainAccess2025,earnings2026}. Field research suggests that such practice could be especially useful in informal settings and for productive skills \parencite{saarela2026}.

Human expertise also remains visible. Learning Designers write prompts and evaluation criteria and select generated content \parencite{contentProduction,duoradio}. A learning-design commentator describes the course expansion as a move towards human--AI collaboration, in which designers become orchestrators and quality guardians, and argues that it can combine scale through technology with quality through human expertise \parencite{hardman2025}. The expansion also added beginner courses for many language pairs that were not previously offered \parencite{courses2025}.

Generation also makes personalisation possible at a level that hand-written content could not reach. Video Call carries facts about the learner from one conversation to the next \parencite{henry2025}, and the learners surveyed by \textcite{catalan2026} asked for precisely this kind of content: scenarios closer to their own work. Access nevertheless remains tiered. Video Call is offered through paid subscriptions \parencite{earnings2026}, so the most interactive practice is still reserved for paying learners.

\subsubsection{The Case for Industrialisation}
The production track is described almost entirely in industrial terms. The reported figures are speed, volume, and cost: about twelve years for 100 courses compared with about one year for nearly 150 \parencite{malikTechcrunch2025}, a fiftyfold increase in DuoRadio episodes with a 99\% cost saving \parencite{duoradio}, and a fall in the cost of a video call from about \$0.30 to under \$0.01, which management linked to offering video calls to Super subscribers \parencite{earnings2026}. The human role shifts from writing content to specifying parameters and selecting from machine output: in the lesson workflow, a designer keeps three of ten generated exercises \parencite{contentProduction}.

What the production figures do not include is evidence about learning quality. The inspected documents on the 148 courses report their number, scope, and speed of creation, but none compares learning outcomes between generated courses and earlier human-authored ones. The only learner-side study of generated lessons found in this review has five respondents and could not confirm which lessons were generated \parencite{catalan2026}; its participants' wish for more personalised, work-relevant scenarios is consistent with the standardisation that template-based generation can produce. External commentators identify the risks. Michael Trucano, a Brookings Institution fellow, warned that rapid generation can produce large amounts of low-quality material, that contextual relevance and alignment with learning objectives are real concerns, that translation alone cannot make materials appropriate across cultures, and that optimising for engagement rather than learning can be detrimental \parencite[Trucano, as cited in][]{toczauer}. Even the favourable learning-design account calls for ethical guardrails that stop algorithms from prioritising engagement at the expense of learning outcomes \parencite{hardman2025}. TechCrunch reported that some users on social media criticised the new content as lower in quality \parencite{malikTechcrunch2025}. Such reports do not measure quality, but they show that quality has become a public question that production figures cannot answer.

\subsubsection{Whose Outcomes Is the System Optimised For?}
The engagement machinery around the generative features predates them and is designed to bring learners back. The notification algorithm defines success as a lesson completed within two hours of a reminder, and reports gains in daily active users and retention \parencite{yancey2020}. An eight-week observational case study of the iOS app identified streaks, streak freezes, social features, push reminders, and layered rewards as mechanisms supporting daily repetition, and concluded that they are highly effective for retention but foster dependence on the app rather than self-sustaining habits \parencite{maass2026}. A critical essay analyses the same design through self-determination theory and dark patterns, arguing that such designs can push learners to engage in order to avoid penalties rather than out of interest \parencite{espinosa2026}. Retention in the app is also not retention of language. In a six-month study of 601 beginners learning Spanish or French, attrition was best predicted by learners' perseverance of effort and age, which suggests that who keeps using the app depends substantially on the learner, not only on its design \parencite{sudina2025}.

These findings point to a tension with learner autonomy. The return mechanics supply external cues, such as reminders and streaks, that tell learners when to practise. A review of AI tools in language education found gains in learner independence mainly when the tools were combined with explicit self-regulation instruction and scaffolding \parencite{mohebbi2025}, which a reminder system does not provide. Generative features could strengthen independence, for instance by explaining errors so that learners can correct themselves, but when they are embedded in mechanics that reward daily return, the more reliably produced result is continued use rather than self-directed learning \parencite{maass2026}.

Company reporting follows the same logic. The figures that accompany the generative features are daily active users, words spoken, content volume, and cost \parencite{earnings2026,shareholderQ12026,duoradio}. DuoRadio illustrates the pattern: a feature intended for listening practice is presented as a success through daily sessions, episode counts, and cost savings, and the inspected account reports no measure of listening improvement \parencite{duoradio}. Learning outcomes are reported occasionally, through company studies lasting about a month (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). This does not show that learners are neglected: more practice can produce more learning, and the company studies do measure learners. It does show which outcomes the system is built to track continuously and which it evaluates only episodically. Table~\ref{tab:ledger} summarises this contrast for each area of the experience.

\subsubsection{An Answer to RQ3}
The evidence does not support choosing one reading over the other. Generative AI enriches the \emph{range} of practice in Duolingo, especially through conversation and explanation, and it industrialises the \emph{production} of content and the delivery of interaction. The two are connected: lower production and inference costs are what allow the enriched features to reach free and cheaper tiers. The imbalance lies in evaluation. The industrial side is measured continuously and reported in precise figures, while the educational side is evaluated occasionally, over short periods, and mostly by the company itself. The system is therefore optimised first for outcomes that can be counted at scale, such as use, retention, and cost, and only secondarily, and less visibly, for learning.

\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion-discussion}
\subsection{Conclusion}
Generative AI transforms the Duolingo user experience in two ways at once. It adds new kinds of interaction, in which learners obtain reasons for their errors and hold open conversations, and it changes how content is produced, so that far more courses and listening episodes can be created quickly and cheaply. The gamified, adaptive structure of the app remains in place, and both changes operate within it.

For RQ1, the features deployed since 2023 are Explain My Answer and Roleplay (March 2023) and Video Call (announced in September 2024), with wider access to both in 2026, alongside generative production of lesson exercises (described in June 2023), DuoRadio episodes (scaled in 2024), and 148 new courses (April 2025). They differ from the pre-generative experience by moving feedback from correctness to explanation and practice from pre-authored selection to open response, while the production changes remain largely invisible to learners.

For RQ2, field-level research shows positive but heterogeneous effects of generative AI on language learning. Duolingo-specific evidence is limited to short, mostly company-authored studies: self-efficacy improved on some items among selected users, speaking performance improved without a gain in confidence in one population, and participation increased. No inspected study directly tests effects on motivation, and most claims about the features' effects are inferred from other settings.

For RQ3, generative AI both enriches and industrialises the experience. The enrichment is real but thinly evaluated, whereas the industrialisation is well documented through speed, volume, and cost. The system is primarily optimised for outcomes measurable at scale, such as daily use, retention, and cost, with learning outcomes evaluated only episodically.

\subsection{Discussion}
\subsubsection{Interpretation}
The most important transformation may be less visible than the conversational features that attract attention. Cheaper generation changes the economics of the app: features that were premium can become standard, and content can be multiplied. Whether this benefits learners depends on quality and learning effects that have not yet been publicly demonstrated. The risk is not that generative AI necessarily makes Duolingo worse, but that success comes to be defined by the metrics that scale most easily.

\subsubsection{Reliability}
Reliability is supported by dated primary documents, citation of the original version of each study, and the separation of announcement, rollout, and access dates. Limitations remain. Selection was purposive rather than exhaustive, no independent second reviewer was involved, and several sources were available only as abstracts or indexed passages. Company webpages can change, so a later reader may find different text. Generative AI assisted with retrieval and drafting, which makes checking against the original sources essential.

\subsubsection{Validity}
The main threat to validity is a mismatch between evidence and claim. It was addressed by classifying specificity and independence and by keeping motivation, confidence, engagement, and performance separate. The enrichment--industrialisation contrast is an interpretive framework; other framings, such as accessibility or cost to learners, could weigh the same evidence differently. An absence of evidence in the inspected sources is reported as such, not as proof that outcomes are poor.

\subsubsection{Usability}
The paper can be used by educators deciding whether and how to recommend Duolingo, by learners interpreting the app's claims, and by developers and researchers planning evaluations. It is not a product ranking and cannot predict how an individual learner will benefit.

\subsubsection{Recommendations}
\emph{Learners and educators} should treat generative features as additional practice rather than proof of progress, combine app use with communication outside the app, and judge progress by tasks the app did not rehearse.

\emph{Duolingo and similar developers} should publish learning-outcome evaluations of generated content, not only production figures; compare generated courses with human-authored ones; report which feature versions were studied; and publish accuracy checks for generated explanations.

\emph{Researchers} should conduct independent, controlled, longer-term studies of confirmed generative features in self-directed app use, measure motivation, confidence, and cognitive engagement separately, and include learners who stop using the app.

\subsubsection{Transparency}
Generative AI tools assisted with source retrieval, organisation, and drafting. The authors are responsible for checking the cited sources and for the content of the paper.
